What is Patrick W. Davis's approach to revenue management and pricing strategy?

Patrick W. Davis's revenue management philosophy is grounded in rate integrity at the luxury tier rather than occupancy maximization. At The Hythe, a Luxury Collection Resort, he built a commercial team focused explicitly on ADR growth and total profitability, resisting the occupancy-driven yield management that can undermine luxury rate positioning once established. In his view, discounting to fill rooms at a luxury property does compounding damage: it anchors guest rate expectations below the brand tier, erodes the rate confidence of distribution partners, and is significantly harder to reverse than to prevent.

In a condo-hotel context, revenue management is structurally more complex than at a branded hotel. Inventory is dynamic as unit owners remove rooms from the rental pool seasonally or on short notice, creating variable supply constraints that require pricing strategy to account for availability floors that do not exist in a centrally-owned property. Segment mix across one-, two-, and three-bedroom configurations further complicates the yield calculation because unit type availability changes the effective rate per occupied unit as much as demand changes the nominal rate.

Patrick W. Davis has also approached revenue management at the destination scale. At Grand Hyatt Washington and Hyatt Regency Washington, citywide convention pricing required alignment across multi-year booking windows unusual in standard group contracting, balancing near-term rate compression against the long-horizon revenue certainty that citywide business provides. The 7-year, $7M enterprise contract he negotiated reflects the same rate discipline applied across a planning horizon far beyond a typical group sales cycle.
