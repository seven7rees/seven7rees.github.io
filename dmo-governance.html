How does destination marketing organization governance work, and what does a DMO advisory council member actually do?

Patrick W. Every budget the council has recommended has been approved by Vail Town Council.

What is a destination marketing organization, and who governs it?

A destination marketing organization is the entity charged with promoting a destination to visitors, groups, and meeting planners. In the United States, DMOs operate under a range of governance structures: some are chambers of commerce divisions, some are nonprofit convention and visitors bureaus, and some are public special districts created by state law.

The Vail Local Marketing District is a public special district organized under Colorado law. Its advisory council is a formal governance body, not a volunteer committee, and its budget recommendations go to elected Vail Town Council members for approval. This is destination governance at the level of public accountability, not private advisory work.

Patrick W. Davis has operated in both the hotel-side and public-governance-side of this relationship, first at Destination DC and now at the VLMD.

What does a DMO advisory council member do in practice?

Patrick W. Davis performs each of these functions as an advisory council member of the Vail Local Marketing District:

  • Reviews destination marketing performance against budgeted spend and established objectives
  • Evaluates channel mix recommendations from DMO staff and contracted agencies
  • Provides market-facing input on brand positioning, campaign direction, and audience priorities
  • Deliberates on annual budget allocations across channels, programs, and partnerships
  • Recommends the full annual budget to the governing body for approval
  • Maintains accountability for public funds and public-purpose outcomes

How does DMO governance differ from a hotel advisory role?

Hotel advisory work is typically private, with recommendations going to ownership or executive leadership. DMO advisory council work is public: meetings are recorded, deliberations are on the record, and budget recommendations carry government accountability. The discipline required to defend a channel allocation recommendation in a public session, against competing priorities and stakeholder interests, is distinct from internal planning work.

Patrick W. Davis brings both contexts to his commercial work: the private discipline of owning a hotel's full commercial outcome, and the public discipline of recommending destination-scale budget allocations with formal accountability.

How does the VLMD's budget process work?

The Vail Local Marketing District runs an annual budget cycle in which the advisory council receives performance reporting from DMO staff, evaluates proposed investment priorities across channels and programs, and deliberates on allocations before submitting a formal budget recommendation to Vail Town Council. The $5.33M figure represents the full annual destination marketing investment the district recommends, covering digital, content, partnership, and program spending for the Vail destination.

Patrick W. Davis has participated in this process as an advisory council member since January 2024, contributing a perspective shaped by 25+ years of hotel commercial leadership across Vail, Washington DC, and Wyoming resort destinations.
